# Sipimals App Store readiness review

Assessment date: 2026-10-17

Scope: source review starting at commit `59e64ec`, inspection of bundled artwork and the built app, model checks, a Release build, and current primary-source research. The app-side readiness changes described here are part of this working tree and are not yet in that starting commit. This is a release-readiness assessment, not legal clearance or a guarantee of App Review approval.

## Finding summary

The current MVP has no obvious feature that is inherently prohibited by Apple’s App Review Guidelines. It is a native, offline hydration log with local history, goal progress, and custom bundled animal artwork. It is closer to submission-ready: the app now links to a bundled privacy-policy draft, includes a `UserDefaults` required-reason manifest, and avoids describing goal credit as measured hydration. Remaining release blockers are a publicly hosted, completed privacy policy; real support contact details; distribution-team signing and App Store Connect setup; and unresolved copyright/trademark clearance. The repository still has no asset provenance/license record, and this review could not compare every screen against the Mobbin reference.

Apple states that all apps must provide a privacy-policy link in App Store Connect and inside the app, and that the policy must explain collection, use, sharing, retention/deletion, and consent withdrawal. See [App Review Guideline 5.1.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#privacy) and [Manage app privacy](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-information/manage-app-privacy/). Settings now links to the bundled draft at [docs/privacy-policy.md](privacy-policy.md), but it still needs the publisher’s legal name and support contact and a public HTTPS URL before release. A support/contact link is not in the app because no real contact details have been provided.

## Policy and product analysis

### Privacy and data disclosure — high priority

The app appears eligible for a “no data collected” App Store privacy response only if the final binary and all linked/embedded code send no user data off-device. Apple defines collection as transmitting data off-device so the developer or partner can access it longer than required to service the request; it also says responses must include third-party partners. See [App Privacy Details](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/) and [User Privacy and Data Use](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/user-privacy-and-data-use/). Verify the archive and any future SDKs before selecting that response.

Remaining actions:

1. Complete and publish [the privacy policy draft](privacy-policy.md) at a public HTTPS URL. It describes drink history, goals and preferences; individual log deletion and uninstall behavior; no account or developer-operated server; and no embedded analytics/ads. It also distinguishes device backups from app cloud synchronization. Add the publisher’s legal name and real support contact; set the public URL in App Store Connect. Settings already links to this bundled policy.
2. Recheck `PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy` in the signed archive. It declares the app-only `UserDefaults` use with `CA92.1`. Apple says uploads using required-reason APIs without an approved reason are not accepted ([required reason API documentation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/describing-use-of-required-reason-api)).
3. Provide a support URL/contact reachable by App Review and customers, add a support link to Settings, and enter the Support URL in App Store Connect. Apple’s [Guideline 1.5](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#developer-information) requires an easy way to contact the developer, and App Store Connect lists Support URL as app-version metadata ([required App Store properties](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-information/required-localizable-and-editable-properties/)).

Because there are no accounts or server data in the current code, a separate account-deletion flow is not required for this MVP. If accounts, sync, analytics, or a server are added, revisit the label, privacy policy, consent, deletion, and security controls.

### Wellness and alcohol — likely acceptable with careful wording

Apple subjects medical apps and apps making health measurement claims to greater scrutiny, requires support for accuracy claims, and says users should be reminded to consult a doctor before medical decisions ([Guideline 1.4.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#physical-harm)). The app’s README, Settings footer, Today label, and VoiceOver now describe this value as goal credit rather than a measure of a user’s hydration level ([README.md:19](../README.md#L19), [PandaSip/Views/SettingsView.swift:67](../PandaSip/Views/SettingsView.swift#L67), [PandaSip/Views/ContentView.swift:144](../PandaSip/Views/ContentView.swift#L144)). Keep store copy and screenshots aligned. Do not market the app as measuring hydration status, preventing illness, diagnosing dehydration, or giving personalized medical advice.

The drink picker logs alcohol at zero goal credit and the app does not sell, recommend, or encourage alcohol. Apple prohibits apps that encourage tobacco, illegal drugs, or excessive alcohol ([Guideline 1.4.3](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#physical-harm)); the current implementation does not appear to do that. Still, alcohol is an explicit App Store age-rating descriptor. Apple defines “Alcohol, Tobacco, or Drug Use or References” to include references or depictions of consumption, and says infrequent references map to a higher age band under the current rating system ([age-rating values and definitions](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-information/age-ratings-values-and-definitions/)). Answer the App Store Connect questionnaire accurately; do not claim “4+” if the alcohol reference and wellness content require a higher result. Avoid alcohol imagery or promotional copy in the icon, screenshots, subtitle, and description.

### Originality, copyright, trademarks, and generated art — evidence gap / high priority

Apple requires that app content be created by the submitter or licensed, and says protected third-party material, misleading or copycat representations, names, and metadata are prohibited ([Guideline 5.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#intellectual-property)). Guideline 4.1 also prohibits copying another app’s name, UI, look and feel, icon, or branding ([Guideline 4.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#copycats); see Apple’s [copycat rejection guidance](https://developer.apple.com/jp/help/app-review/guideline-reference/4-1-prevent-copycat-rejection)).

At the time of this review, the app used the “Panda Sip” name, a panda/cup icon, and seven selectable animal illustrations. Runtime source contains no Waterllama, Mobbin, or Studio Ghibli branding. Visually inspected artwork covers all seven selectable animals and the current icon. A limited comparison with Waterllama's [official character promotional image](https://waterllama.com/images/water-drinking-app.png), linked from its [official site](https://waterllama.com/), shows materially different rendering: the app has textured, rounded characters holding a separate glass; the reference uses flatter illustrated animal characters. This is a visual observation, not a finding of non-infringement. Shared drink logging, streaks and animals alone do not establish copied expression. The U.S. Copyright Office distinguishes ideas/methods from protected expression and notes that names may raise trademark issues ([Circular 33](https://copyright.gov/circs/circ33.pdf)). Apple's copycat review can still assess the overall product independently of a copyright claim.

The exact Mobbin URL failed to load, and the connected Mobbin MCP search returned a paid-plan requirement. No competitor art was added to this repository. A full flow comparison, reverse-image search, trademark search, and jurisdiction-specific legal review remain undone. Search “Sipimals,” “Panda Sip,” similar spellings, and similar marks in the intended markets; a GitHub name, available bundle ID, or available App Store title is not a trademark clearance result. The [USPTO trademark search guidance](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search) is a starting point for U.S. clearance, not coverage of Canada or every release territory.

Before submission, retain a rights ledger for the seven `*Cup` illustrations, their 14 derived `*RigHead` and `*RigBody` crops, `AppIcon.png`, the older bundled `PandaStorybook` illustration, and any store screenshot/marketing elements. Record creator/tool, source inputs and their permissions, generation date, applicable license/terms, human edits, and the exact final file. The old illustration needs a record too if it remains bundled, even though it is not selectable. Preserve the generation records from this conversation. Do not imply that selecting a studio-inspired style grants permission to reproduce a studio's characters or artwork, and avoid studio/competitor branding in marketing.

For OpenAI-generated assets, check the terms that applied to the account or organization at creation time. Current individual terms assign Output rights as between OpenAI and the user, subject to applicable law, but warn that output may not be unique and place responsibility for inputs/use on the user ([OpenAI Terms of Use](https://openai.com/policies/terms-of-use/)). Contractual output rights do not establish non-infringement or exclusive statutory copyright. In the U.S., human-authored contributions may qualify for copyright while purely AI-generated expression may not; prompts alone generally are insufficient ([U.S. Copyright Office, January 2025 findings](https://www.copyright.gov/newsnet/2025/1060.html)). These U.S. findings do not settle the law in Canada or every intended market. A qualified IP review of the actual assets and name is the remaining route to stronger legal assurance.

### Functionality, metadata, and review access

The app has a persistent, native utility: it logs drinks, preserves history, calculates disclosed approximate credit, supports deletion, and works locally. That is materially stronger than a web wrapper and aligns with [Guideline 4.2 Minimum Functionality](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#minimum-functionality). Do not submit it as a “reminder” app while reminders are absent; the current README accurately calls reminders outside the MVP.

Before review, Apple requires a final, functional binary, accurate metadata, working URLs, on-device testing, and access to all features; see [Before You Submit](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#before-you-submit) and [Guideline 2.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#app-completeness). Provide review notes explaining that animal rewards are earned from local qualifying-day history, that alcohol entries are retained but contribute zero goal credit, and how the reviewer can test all animal states without waiting 90 days. Use screenshots of the actual app in use; Apple’s [Guideline 2.3](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#accurate-metadata) requires accurate metadata and screenshots and prohibits irrelevant trademarked terms or unverifiable claims.

The app’s cute animal presentation may appeal to children, but the repository does not establish that it is intended for Apple’s Kids Category. Do not use “for kids” or “for children” in name, subtitle, icon, screenshots, or description unless you intentionally enter the Kids Category and meet its requirements; Apple reserves those terms for that category ([Guideline 5.1.4](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#kids) and [Guideline 2.3.8](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#accurate-metadata)).

### Future paid animals — policy constraint

The current giraffe, monkey, and pig rewards are earned through qualifying local streaks and have no purchase flow. No purchase-related issue was found in this implementation. If animals are sold later, StoreKit In-App Purchase is the standard route. Apple also has storefront-specific external-purchase exceptions; evaluate those separately if needed rather than assuming a universal ban or permission ([Guidelines 3.1.1 and 3.1.1(a)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#payments)). Update review materials when the business model changes.

The built app contains `PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy` and `privacy-policy.md`, with no embedded `.framework` or `.appex`. This is not signed archive/Organizer validation and not a traffic-capture audit.
- The phone's test unlocks were seeded in device preferences in the prior installation task. No unlock-all testing branch was found in source, so the production code still enforces 30/60/90 qualifying days.
- This readiness pass added the required-reason manifest, bundled privacy-policy view and Settings link, and non-medical goal-credit wording. It also aligned the project bundle ID with the existing phone test build. The updated Debug build was installed and launched on the paired iPhone; no app data was intentionally reset. No App Store Connect settings were changed.

## Release gate

Before uploading, resolve these in order:

1. Add the publisher’s legal name and real support contact to the policy, host it publicly over HTTPS, add a Settings support link, and enter the Privacy Policy and Support URLs in App Store Connect.
2. Confirm the paid distribution team can use `com.philiphe.pandasip`, archive-sign with that team, and recheck the privacy manifest and archive contents. Scan the final archive and network behavior for unexpected SDKs or data transmission.
3. Complete current App Store Connect age-rating, content-rights, privacy, export-compliance, category, copyright, and metadata fields. Declare alcohol references honestly and avoid Kids Category language unless opting into that category.
4. Record the artwork sources and applicable terms; perform trademark and visual-confusion checks for “Sipimals” and the animal artwork. This remains an unresolved rights review, not a confirmed infringement finding.
5. Test the signed build on physical iPhones. Verify all seven animals, history deletion, storage failure behavior, VoiceOver, Dynamic Type, Reduce Motion, and the external study links. Submit with review notes explaining local streak rewards and zero goal credit for alcohol entries. The unsigned Release build establishes compilation, not App Store upload acceptance.
